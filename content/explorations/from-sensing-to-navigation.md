Neurofly starts with a question: can navigation emerge from an olfactory processing loop instead of a scripted route?

## Keep the chain visible

The intended chain moves from an odor environment to receptor activation, neural response, a decision signal, and movement. Each stage changes how information is represented. Keeping those stages separate makes it easier to ask which part of the loop is shaping behaviour.

## A simulation is a place to ask questions

Competing odor sources and bilateral sensing are part of the project direction. They offer ways to explore how local sensing might influence movement without treating a visually interesting path as proof of biological accuracy.

## Toward physical embodiment

An ESP32-based robot is a future direction. Moving from a virtual concentration field to physical sensors introduces a different sensing problem. The shared control idea is the starting point; equivalent behaviour has not been demonstrated here.

This note describes the builder's supplied research question and intended architecture. It reports no experimental result or completed robot integration.
